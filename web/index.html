BTL Photo Printer Bridge

Print straight from your camera: press SET on a photo and it comes out of the printer. Tested with a Canon EOS R6 Mark II and a Canon SELPHY CP1500.

Step 1: Plug in the board

Plug the ESP32-S3 board into this computer with a USB cable, using the port marked UART (or COM).

Board doesn't light up? That port often needs a USB-A to USB-C cable; a USB-C to USB-C cable may not power it.

Step 2: Click Install

Pick the board in the list and click Connect, then follow the prompts.

The board is usually called USB Single Serial, CH340, CH343 or CP210x. Not sure? Click Cancel, unplug the board, click Install again: the one that disappeared is your board.

This page only works in Chrome or Edge on a computer. Open this page over https to install.

Asked to "Erase device"? First install: tick it. Updating a bridge you already set up: leave it unticked to keep your settings.

Stuck on connecting? Hold the board's BOOT button while you click Install.

Step 3: Connect it to your Wi-Fi

Right after installing, this page asks for your Wi-Fi. Pick your network, type the password and click Connect. Use the same 2.4 GHz network as your printer.

When it says it's connected, click Visit Device to open the bridge's page. It shows the bridge's IP address (you'll type it into the camera), the printer and the activity log. Later you can also open http://btl-photo.local.

Already installed? Click Install, connect, and choose Change Wi-Fi (or Visit Device to find its address).

No Wi-Fi prompt? The bridge makes its own Wi-Fi network called BTL-Photo-Setup. Join it from your phone and go to http://192.168.4.1 to enter your Wi-Fi there.

Step 4: Connect the printer

Over Wi-Fi: nothing to do if the printer is on the same Wi-Fi. The bridge finds it by itself.

Over a USB cable (faster to start printing): board port USB → USB-C to USB-A adapter → USB-A to USB-C cable → printer.

  • Bridge the board's OTG solder pads so its USB port sends 5 V.
  • Use a USB-A to USB-C cable that your computer recognises the printer with. Some cheap ones are missing a small resistor the printer needs.
  • A plain USB-C to USB-C cable won't work for this.

Step 5: Set up the camera (Canon FTP transfer)

Now press Playback, pick a photo and press SET. It prints.

Tip: reserve the bridge's IP address in your router so the camera always finds it.

Print modes and settings

Print every photo the camera sends, or only photos rated 1–5 stars or more (rate in the camera, then send a batch). Photos are shrunk to the printer's resolution and turned upright before printing; Fast mode sends a lighter file that starts printing sooner.
