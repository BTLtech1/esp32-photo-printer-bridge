BTL Photo Printer Bridge

Print straight from your camera: press SET on a photo and it comes out of the printer. Tested with a Canon EOS R6 Mark II and a Canon SELPHY CP1500.

Step 1: Plug in the board

Plug the ESP32-S3 board into this computer with a USB cable, using the port marked UART (or COM).

Board doesn't light up? That port often needs a USB-A to USB-C cable; a USB-C to USB-C cable may not power it.

Step 2: Click Install

Pick the board in the list and click Connect, then follow the prompts.

The board is usually called USB Single Serial, CH340, CH343 or CP210x. Not sure? Click Cancel, unplug the board, click Install again: the one that disappeared is your board.

This page only works in Chrome or Edge on a computer. Open this page over https to install.

Asked to "Erase device"? First install: tick it. Updating a bridge you already set up: leave it unticked to keep your settings.

Stuck on connecting? Hold the board's BOOT button while you click Install.

Step 3: Wi-Fi

The bridge runs its own Wi-Fi network, BTL-Photo (password printphoto). The camera connects to that, and the bridge's page is at http://192.168.4.1 on it. For printing over the USB cable that's all you need, at home or in the field.

Right after installing, this page also offers to join your home Wi-Fi (2.4 GHz). Optional, but handy: the bridge can then reach a printer on your Wi-Fi, and you can open its page from your normal network at http://btl-photo.local. Once it's connected, the dialog shows that address next to the bridge's name.

Stuck on "Wrapping up"? The firmware is already installed; the browser just couldn't reopen the port. Reload this page, click Install again, pick the port, and choose Connect to Wi-Fi.

Already installed? Click Install and connect: the dialog shows the version on the board and offers Update when a newer one is available, plus Change Wi-Fi. Give the board half a minute after an install before connecting again.

No Wi-Fi prompt at all? Join BTL-Photo from your phone, open http://192.168.4.1, and enter your home Wi-Fi under "Home Wi-Fi".

Step 4: Connect the printer

Over Wi-Fi: nothing to do if the printer is on the same Wi-Fi. The bridge finds it by itself.

Over a USB cable (faster to start printing): board port USB → USB-C to USB-A adapter → USB-A to USB-C cable → printer.

  • Bridge the board's OTG solder pads so its USB port sends 5 V.
  • Use a USB-A to USB-C cable that your computer recognises the printer with. Some cheap ones are missing a small resistor the printer needs.
  • A plain USB-C to USB-C cable won't work for this.

Now press Playback, pick a photo and press SET. It prints.

Using your home Wi-Fi instead? Then the address is the one on the bridge's page; reserve it in your router so it doesn't change.

Print modes and settings

Print every photo the camera sends, or only photos rated 1–5 stars or more (rate in the camera, then send a batch). Photos are shrunk to the printer's native print size before printing, so it has nothing left to scale. Photos that aren't 3:2 are trimmed to fill the paper, unless you turn Fill the paper off.

Your bridge's own page (printer, activity log, settings): http://btl-photo.local or its IP address.
